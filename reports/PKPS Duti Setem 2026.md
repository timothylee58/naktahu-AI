# PKPS Duti Setem 2026 waives penalties, not duty

PKPS (Program Khas Pengakuan Sukarela Duti Setem 2026, the Stamp Duty Special Voluntary Disclosure Programme) is a **penalty waiver run by LHDN/HASiL, not a stamp duty exemption**. It removes the late-stamping and late-payment penalties on instruments **executed between 1 January 2023 and 31 December 2025** that were never stamped or never paid. The duty itself is still payable in full. The Prime Minister announced it on 5 January 2026 for 1 January to 30 June 2026, and HASiL extended it on 26 June 2026 to **31 December 2026** ([HASiL media statement](https://www.hasil.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/20260626-kenyataan-media-hasil-lanjutan-pkps-duti-setem-sehingga-31-dis-2026.pdf)). As of 27 November 2026, about five weeks remain. No further extension had been announced, and LHDN's CEO has repeatedly said the programme ends this year ([Malay Mail, 31 Oct 2026](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/10/31/stamp-duty-and-e-invoice-amnesty-windows-are-closing-irb-reminds-taxpayers/241417)). Applicants must submit through e-Duti Setem on MyTax, and both stamping and payment must be completed by 31 December. Instruments regularised this way are protected from stamp duty audit. PKPS is distinct from the first-home stamp duty exemption and from the Budget 2027 proposals, which are covered separately below. **Caveat:** no hasil.gov.my page could be fetched directly. Every HASiL claim below comes from search-engine excerpts of the official PDFs, not from reading them in full.

## A s.47A penalty remission, not a duty relief

PKPS is a remission of penalties under section 47A(2) of the Stamp Act 1949. HASiL's operating guideline is titled "Garis Panduan Operasi Layanan Khas Penalti di bawah Seksyen 47A Akta Setem 1949", was issued under s.76B, and was updated on 29 June 2026 ([HASiL guideline](https://www.hasil.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/garis-panduan-operasi-layanan-khas-penalti-di-bawah-seksyen-47a-akta-setem-1949-bagi-program-khas-pengakuan-sukarela-duti-setem-2026-1.pdf)). The announcement came from the Prime Minister's New Year address on 5 January 2026 ([Donovan & Ho](https://dnh.com.my/missed-your-stamping-deadline-heres-how-to-get-a-late-stamping-penalty-waiver-under-pkps-2026/)). The extension came days before the 30 June expiry, with Ministry of Finance approval. HASiL tied it to the transition to the Sistem Taksir Sendiri Duti Setem (STSDS, the self-assessment system) ([Bernama, 26 Jun 2026](https://bernama.com/bm/news.php?id=2573386); [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/06/malaysia-stamp-duty-extension-of-special-voluntary-disclosure-programme)).

The mechanics matter for anyone expecting a discount. Duty is computed at ordinary First Schedule rates first, and only the penalty is then removed. The penalties at stake are the greater of RM50 or 10% of duty if stamping is within three months after the deadline, and the greater of RM100 or 20% otherwise. The normal stamping deadline is 30 days from execution in Malaysia, or from first receipt in Malaysia if signed abroad ([HASiL guideline](https://www.hasil.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/garis-panduan-operasi-layanan-khas-penalti-di-bawah-seksyen-47a-akta-setem-1949-bagi-program-khas-pengakuan-sukarela-duti-setem-2026-1.pdf); [Donovan & Ho](https://dnh.com.my/missed-your-stamping-deadline-heres-how-to-get-a-late-stamping-penalty-waiver-under-pkps-2026/)). The guideline's worked examples show the effect. An instrument dated 1 December 2025 with RM80,000 duty and a RM16,000 penalty costs RM80,000 if paid by 31 December 2026, and RM96,000 if paid on 2 January 2027. An instrument dated 1 April 2022 gets no relief, and its RM1,600 penalty on RM8,000 duty stays payable. An instrument dated 12 January 2026 pays RM1,100 on RM1,000 duty, because 2026 instruments are outside the programme. No property-price band, percentage exemption or cap exists, because there is no duty reduction to cap.

We did not find a gazetted Exemption Order, P.U.(A) number or Finance Act provision for PKPS. The operational-guideline route suggests none exists, but this is an inference.

## Coverage is set by execution date, not by who you are

PKPS covers **all types of instruments** executed 1 January 2023 to 31 December 2025 that are unstamped or whose duty is unpaid. That includes tenancy agreements, loan and financing agreements, SPAs, transfers and commercial contracts ([HASiL FAQ, 28 Jan 2026](https://www.hasil.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/soalan-lazim-faq-pengecualian-penalti-program-pengakuan-khas-sukarela-pkps-duti-setem-2026.pdf)). It is open to all duty payers, including companies, law firms, company secretaries, agencies and non-citizens ([HASiL article, Nanyang Siang Pau](https://www.hasil.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/20261014-Nanyang-Siang-Pau-An-Opportunity-To-Settle-Outstanding-Stamp-Duty-Without-Penalties.pdf)). No citizenship, first-home or developer condition applies.

An instrument stamped before 1 January 2026 but with duty or penalty still unpaid remains eligible if payment lands in the window. An instrument whose duty and penalty were both fully paid before 1 January 2026 is not eligible and gets no refund (guideline paras 4.5 to 4.6, per the same sources). Three groups are excluded: fraud or forgery intended to evade duty, instruments dated before 2023, and instruments dated 2026 onward. Instruments paid after 31 December 2026 are also outside the programme.

On audit, instruments stamped under PKPS will not be audited, but other unstamped instruments stay exposed to the Stamp Duty Audit Framework. An adviser reports that the DGIR said audits generally reach back about three years, against a five-year statutory time bar. This was verbal and non-binding, so pre-2023 instruments remain legally dutiable ([Zic Legal](https://www.ziclegal.com/resources/regularising-the-past-the-2026-stamp-duty-voluntary-disclosure-program)). Baker McKenzie notes a discretionary general voluntary-disclosure route that can reduce penalty to 10%, but only when no audit is underway.

We found no clawback provision beyond the fraud exclusion. Whether a granted waiver could later be reversed is unverified.

## Applying means filing through e-Duti Setem and paying by 31 December

No separate PKPS application or penalty appeal exists. The route is to submit the instrument online through MyTax (https://mytax.hasil.gov.my), then Perkhidmatan ezHasil, Duti Setem, e-Duti Setem. Manual filing is not accepted, and the old STAMPS system was retired on 31 December 2025 ([HASiL e-Buletin Edisi 2 2026](https://www.hasil.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/e-Buletin-HASiL-Edisi-2_2026.pdf)). The steps follow HASiL's STSDS procedure ([HASiL media statement, 17 Dec 2025](https://www.hasil.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/20251217-kenyataan-media-hasil-sistem-e-duti-setem-boleh-akses-lebih-awal-melalui-mytax-mulai-17-dis-2025.pdf)):

1. Log in with a TIN (register through e-Daftar if you have none) and choose the individual or agent role.
2. Select the Borang Nyata Duti Setem (BNDS) type, fill in the details and attach the instrument.
3. Check the real-time duty computation and submit.
4. Pay within the stipulated period.
5. Receive the stamp duty certificate once HASiL confirms payment, and keep the instrument and records for seven years.

The notice or BNDS may still display a penalty, but it appears as "Penalty Waived" and is excluded from the total payable ([HASiL FAQ (EN)](https://www.hasil.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/FAQ_Penalty_Exemption_Under_The_Special_Voluntary_Disclosure_Programme_PKPS_For_Stamp_Duty_2026.pdf)).

The timing mechanics differ by instrument type. Under STSDS Phase 1 (since 1 January 2026), tenancy/lease, securities and general instruments are self-assessed. Submitting the BNDS counts as the assessment, and payment is due within 30 days of that date. Property transfers without JPPH valuation stay on formal assessment, meaning HASiL issues a Notice of Assessment, until self-assessment begins on 1 January 2027. Other instruments follow on 1 January 2028 ([HASiL operational guideline, 26 Dec 2025](https://www.hasil.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/20251226-garis-panduan-operasi-permohonan-penyeteman-melalui-sistem-taksir-sendiri-duti-setem.pdf)). Because the waiver tests payment rather than submission, a late-December filing that needs a Notice of Assessment is risky. HASiL, EY and Deloitte all advise filing early ([EY](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-my/technical/tax-alerts/documents/ey-stamp-duty-special-voluntary-disclosure-program-06-26.pdf)). The helpline is HCC 03-8911 1000. A per-instrument document checklist was not found. HASiL's examples are the instrument plus related documents, such as the SPA, title or Form KTN14A for transfers, and the loan agreement plus offer letter for securities.

## Keep PKPS separate from first-home relief and Budget 2027

Three measures are easily confused, and only the first is PKPS. PKPS waives penalties on late instruments. The first-home exemption, by contrast, reduces duty itself. Budget 2026 (tabled 10 October 2025) extended a full stamp duty exemption on transfer instruments and loan agreements for first-time buyers of homes up to **RM500,000** to 31 December 2027 ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2025/10/10/budget-2026-stamp-duty-exemption-extended-for-first-time-homebuyers)). A third item is a set of Budget 2027 (9 October 2026) **proposals**. They would exempt first homes up to RM500,000 in full, and for homes up to RM750,000 would exempt the first RM500,000 plus 50% of the remainder. They would apply to SPAs executed from 1 January 2027 to 31 December 2030 ([The Star, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2026/10/09/budget-2027-govt-proposes-stamp-duty-exemptions-for-first-time-homebuyers); [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/amp/news/malaysia/2026/10/09/budget-2027-stamp-duty-exemption-proposed-for-first-homes-up-to-rm500000/238432)). These are not verified as gazetted, and a PwC snippet cited in the notes describing the RM750,000 measure was not verified either. None of these conditions applies to PKPS. Whether a late first-home instrument can combine a duty exemption with the PKPS penalty waiver is unverified. The gazetted exemption order was not read.

## Conclusion

The practical point is that PKPS has no ceiling on what it can save and no price band, because it is not a duty cut. Its value is the penalty avoided, at most 20% of duty with a RM100 floor, and the audit protection that comes with regularising an instrument. The decision therefore turns on one test, whether payment, not submission, can be completed by 31 December 2026.

The main risk is not eligibility but timing and system load, since property transfers on formal assessment need a Notice of Assessment before payment is possible. Anyone holding unstamped 2023 to 2025 instruments should file now, and should not treat the Budget 2027 first-home proposals as relevant to this deadline.
