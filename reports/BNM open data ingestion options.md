# Ingest BNM facts, not BNM firehoses

NakTahu AI should ingest Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) data as a small set of curated, self-describing "fact" chunks in `document_chunks`. Full time-series history should live in a relational table and be reached by structured lookup, not by embeddings. The core series are the Overnight Policy Rate (OPR), exchange rates, Kijang Emas gold prices and per-bank base lending rates. Third-party wrappers describe all four as sourced from BNM's Open API ([oanor spec](https://oanor.com/api/malaysia-fx-api/openapi.json)). Narrative material such as Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) statements should be cited and quoted briefly, not bulk-copied. **Important caveat: the researchers could not reach any BNM host (data.bnm.gov.my, api.bnm.gov.my, www.bnm.gov.my all failed DNS resolution in the sandbox), so no endpoint, header, authentication, rate limit or robots.txt detail has been verified.** The only licence evidence comes from search snippets of BNM's terms pages, and those snippets point to a workable regime: BNM datasets are free to reuse if labelled as BNM-sourced, while general website content is "all rights reserved". The design recommended below limits index growth by construction, and every item that depends on unverified facts is gated behind the checklist at the end of this report.

## What is verified, what is snippet-only, and what is memory

Almost nothing here was read from a primary page, so the evidence has three tiers. The first tier is "snippet-seen": a search-engine summary of a real page, which suggests the page exists but was not read in full. The second tier is "third-party description": a vendor or wrapper describing BNM. The third tier is "memory": the researchers' prior knowledge, with no source. Nothing reached the stronger tier of a primary page read in full.

The practical consequence is that the endpoint list, the required Accept header, any API-key requirement, pagination, date parameters, per-series update times and rate limits are all hypotheses. Several of the third-party wrappers (Zyla, Apify, the oanor vendor key header) are explicitly not official and should not be used as ingestion sources, because they add an unlicensed intermediary and break the repo's rule that citations point to real `gov.my`-family URLs. The one robust fallback is data.gov.my, which republishes BNM exchange rates and interest rates, though its own licence also needs checking.

## Licence and access: a permissive dataset regime beside a restrictive website regime

BNM's terms appear to split in two. According to a search snippet, the BNM Datasets terms permit free use, copying, publishing and distribution provided the material is labelled as BNM-sourced, and third-party datasets inside that portal fall under the standard terms ([Dataset terms](https://www.bnm.gov.my/terms-of-use-bnm-datasets)). In contrast, the general Terms of Use bar uploading copyright material without the owner's permission, and footers read "© Bank Negara Malaysia ... All rights reserved" ([Terms of Use](https://www.bnm.gov.my/terms-of-use)). The Malaysia International Islamic Financial Centre (MIFC) portal is stricter again: no copying, redistribution or commercial exploitation without permission ([MIFC disclaimer](https://www.bnm.gov.my/web/mifc/disclaimer)). The full text of none of these was read.

The reading that follows is an inference, not legal advice. Numeric series obtained through the Open API or the datasets catalogue are likely reusable with a "Source: Bank Negara Malaysia" attribution. This matters for NakTahu's metered Developer/Knowledge API, because redistributing BNM numbers to paying API customers is a stronger use than showing them in an answer. Whether the dataset terms cover that redistribution, and whether they cover derived summaries such as monthly FX means, has to be confirmed in the licence text itself. MPC statements, the Annual Report, Financial Stability Review (FSR) and Quarterly Bulletin sit under the general all-rights-reserved regime. For those, the safe posture is brief quotation with a link-back, or written permission from BNM before full-text ingestion.

Access constraints are the least known area. Rate limits, API-key requirements, robots.txt for both api.bnm.gov.my and data.bnm.gov.my, and any WAF behaviour on www.bnm.gov.my were not observed. Until they are, the polite default is a single-threaded fetcher with a descriptive User-Agent, aggressive caching (most series update at most daily) and backoff on 429/5xx. BNM also offers no RSS feed as far as the snippets show, only an "Email Alert" subscription ([Alert](https://www.bnm.gov.my/alert)), so freshness for narrative material needs a change-watcher rather than a feed reader.

## Narrative sources are predictable and stable enough to watch

For non-API material, the snippets reveal URL structures that suit a lightweight watcher. MPC statements use date-coded slugs of the form `/-/monetary-policy-statement-DDMMYYYY`, with 2026 meetings on 22 Jan, 5 Mar, 7 May, 9 Jul, 3 Sep and 5 Nov, released at 3:00 pm; the OPR decisions table lives at `/monetary-stability/opr-decisions` ([OPR decisions](https://www.bnm.gov.my/web/guest/monetary-stability/opr-decisions), [5 Nov statement](https://www.bnm.gov.my/-/monetary-policy-statement-05112026)). The OPR is reported at 2.75% from 22 January through at least 3 September 2026; the 5 November decision value was not seen, so the knowledge base must not hard-code a "current" OPR from these notes. Financial Consumer Alert updates use `/-/fca-update-YYMMDD` slugs ([FCA update](https://www.bnm.gov.my/-/fca-update-030826)), and press releases sit under `/press-releases` ([Press releases](https://www.bnm.gov.my/press-releases)).

Periodic publications live under `/publications`. The latest FSR seen is Second Half 2025 (31 March 2026), BNM says it is moving to an annual FSR, and the print Quarterly Bulletin has ended, with 4Q 2025 listed on 13 February 2026 ([FSR](https://www.bnm.gov.my/publications/fsr2025h2), [Quarterly Bulletin](https://www.bnm.gov.my/quarterly-bulletin-2025)). Monthly Highlights and Statistics editions exist as individual pages covering the banking system, financial markets, insurance/takaful and the external sector ([Monthly Highlights](https://www.bnm.gov.my/-/monthly-highlights-statistics-in-june-2025-1)). Formats (HTML, PDF, XLSX) are confirmed only for the FSR XLSX datapack. Two things the notes could not locate: a BNM policy-document listing page and any sitemap.xml. A Monthly Highlights and Statistics edition is a candidate source for series outside the four confirmed ones, since it covers banking and money-market content, but whether it is machine-readable is unknown.

## A design that cannot flood the index

The retrieval literature gives a clear warning, though not a hard threshold. Near-duplicate chunks waste top-k slots and reduce diversity ([Ragnarok](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.16828), [Chunk Filtering](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24334)), and dense retrieval cannot distinguish old and new versions of the same fact without an explicit time signal ([Solving Freshness in RAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19376v1)). The evidence on how much deduplication helps is mixed: one project removed only 2-4% near-duplicates with minimal effect ([CS6101 project](https://wing-nus.github.io/cs6101/files/STePS-2025/CS6101-04.pdf)), so the real risk is quality of ranking, not storage. Nobody tested per-observation versus per-period versus latest-card designs head to head, so the structure below is the researchers' inference, not a benchmarked result.

The design has three chunk types and one rule. A latest-value card holds one chunk per series, for example "Overnight Policy Rate (OPR), Bank Negara Malaysia: 2.75% per annum, as of <date>; previous <value> (changed <date>). Source: <series URL>", replaced in place on update. A change-event chunk covers each step of a rarely changing series such as OPR, one per MPC decision, linking to that statement. A per-period summary chunk covers dense series: one per currency pair per month with open, close, high, low, mean, unit ("MYR per 1 USD") and observation count. The rule is that daily history never enters the vector index; it stays in a relational table and is queried by SQL, which also fits the finding that aggregations need whole tables and cannot be answered from partial chunks ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/chunking-strategies-for-structured-data-in-rag-systems)). A few dozen series then yield low thousands of chunks (the notes' sizing guess, unsourced).

Retrieval-time controls close the remaining gap. Metadata on each chunk should include `series_id`, `as_of_date`, `observation_period`, `unit`, `source_url`, `is_current`, `valid_to` and `content_hash`, with the hash covering normalised value text plus the as-of date, so an unchanged daily refetch inserts nothing and a revision inserts a new row and supersedes the old. Undated queries filter `is_current`; dated queries filter by `as_of_date` range rather than trusting embeddings, which separate months and numbers like "0.3 vs 0.30" poorly (inference). Cap results per `series_id` and apply MMR so thirty monthly FX summaries cannot crowd out other evidence. Put both English and Bahasa Malaysia aliases in chunk text ("kadar dasar semalaman", "Mac 2026") so the BM25 side of hybrid search matches bilingual queries. This design does not alter the `analyst_node` confidence check.

Three repo-specific safeguards follow from CLAUDE.md. First, per Trap #14, only `document_chunks` affects live answers; `dosm_documents` does not, so BNM facts must be written to the former with `content_hash` dedup. Second, every chunk passes the mandatory `_fold_confusables(NFKC(text))` scan against `INJECTION_PATTERNS`. Numbers rendered by a fixed template from typed API fields carry low injection risk (still range-checked, e.g. rate 0-20, FX above 0). Third-party prose such as MPC statements and press releases is the real exposure, since poisoning attacks reached about 90% success with five malicious texts in one study ([PoisonedRAG](https://usenix.org/system/files/usenixsecurity25-zou-poisonedrag.pdf)) and defences are only partial; the notes suggest quarantining scan hits, restricting chunk URL hosts to `bnm.gov.my` and `data.gov.my`, and never ingesting API free-text fields unscanned. Third, the 3600s query-result cache key excludes data freshness, so a cached OPR answer can outlive an MPC decision. The notes propose a corpus-version stamp in the cache key or a `finance`-domain flush on ingest; this is an inference from repo rules and needs a decision from the maintainers. Citations must be the exact series, dataset or MPC statement page; a bare bnm.gov.my homepage is not acceptable, and if an API endpoint needs a key, cite the human-readable page instead. Derived values (changes, percent moves, averages) should be computed in code, not by the LLM, since financial table QA shows hallucination and unit-scale errors ([Confidently Wrong](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.11414)).

## Conclusion

The central finding is that the licence question and the flooding question have the same answer: ingest few, high-value, attributable facts and keep bulk history out of the vector index. That serves the permissive dataset terms (attribution is easy when each chunk carries a series URL), avoids the all-rights-reserved narrative regime (quote briefly, link back), and keeps top-k free of near-duplicates. The decision that most needs the maintainers is the cache-invalidation policy for time-sensitive answers, because it affects the live trust layer more than the ingestion code does.

The honest status is that no ingestion code should be written yet. The endpoint shapes are remembered, not observed, and the licence text, robots.txt and rate limits are unread. GitHub Actions runners can reach .gov.my, so the unknowns can be resolved in one short job. If the BNM API turns out to be restricted or undocumented, data.gov.my is the fallback for FX and interest rates, subject to its own licence.

## Verification checklist (run from a GitHub Actions runner before any ingestion code)

1. Resolve and fetch `https://api.bnm.gov.my/portal`, `https://data.bnm.gov.my/`, and `https://www.bnm.gov.my/`; record status codes, redirects and any WAF or "client challenge" behaviour.
2. Download `robots.txt` and `sitemap.xml` for all three hosts; record any Disallow rules, Crawl-delay or automated-access statements.
3. Save the full text of `/terms-of-use`, `/terms-of-use-bnm-datasets` and the MIFC disclaimer. Confirm the exact attribution wording, and whether redistribution through a paid API and derived summaries are covered.
4. Pull the API portal's endpoint list or OpenAPI document. Confirm the base URL, the Accept header, whether an API key or registration is needed, pagination, date parameters, and the response envelope.
5. Call the OPR, exchange-rate, Kijang Emas and base-rate endpoints once each. Record update times, `meta` fields, and any rate-limit headers (`Retry-After`, `X-RateLimit-*`); then send a short burst to find the 429 threshold.
6. Check whether KLIBOR, MGS yields, M1/M3 and banking statistics exist in the API, on data.bnm.gov.my (CSV/Excel export), or only in Monthly Highlights and Statistics.
7. Fetch `/monetary-stability/opr-decisions` and the 5 Nov 2026 MPC statement; confirm the current OPR value, the HTML versus PDF format, and the slug pattern.
8. Check the data.gov.my catalogue entries for exchangerates and interestrates: licence, update cadence, API access, and the BNM source labelling.
9. Confirm that every series page URL intended for citation resolves with HTTP 200 and sits on a `gov.my` host.
10. Run a sample of fetched API free-text and MPC prose through `_fold_confusables(NFKC(text))` and `INJECTION_PATTERNS` to confirm the scan path works on this content.
