# e-Invoice (MyInvois) Phased Rollout

- Phase 1 (1 Aug 2024): businesses with annual revenue > RM100 million
- Phase 2 (1 Jan 2025): businesses with annual revenue RM25 million–RM100 million
- Phase 3 (1 Jul 2025): businesses with annual revenue RM500,000–RM25 million
- Phase 4 (1 Jan 2026): businesses with annual revenue RM150,000–RM500,000 (this phase
  was itself extended from an original 1 Jul 2025 date to give ~240,000 SMEs more time)
- The RM1 million sub-threshold within Phase 4 was reset in Dec 2025 — businesses
  straddling this line should re-check their applicable date, not assume last year's phase.
- Subsidiaries, or companies with a non-individual shareholder or related-company/JV
  partner earning ≥ RM1 million/year, must comply from 1 Jul 2026 regardless of their
  own standalone revenue.
- Non-compliant invoices are not legally recognised for tax-deduction purposes by the
  receiving party — this creates B2B pressure even before enforcement penalties apply.
- Penalty for non-compliance after enforcement begins: fines up to RM20,000 per offence,
  and/or imprisonment up to 6 months.

# Annual Income Tax Filing (Forms)

- Form BE (employment income only, no business income): due 30 Apr (manual) / 15 May (e-Filing)
- Form B (business/self-employment income): due 30 Jun (manual) / 15 Jul (e-Filing)
- Form E (employer's annual return — list of employees & remuneration): due 31 Mar
- Form EA (employee's annual remuneration statement, given to staff, not filed): due 28 Feb
- Form C (company tax return): due within 7 months of financial year end (e-Filing: 8 months)
- Companies must also note the company-specific deadline derived from their own
  financial-year-end — this is NOT a fixed calendar date like BE/B forms.

# Late Filing Penalties

- Late filing: 10% penalty on unpaid tax, rising to 15% if unpaid beyond 60 days
- Separately, a fine of RM200–RM20,000, and/or imprisonment up to 6 months, may apply
  for failing to file on time (this is in addition to the percentage penalty above)
- Individuals with annual employment income below RM37,333 (after EPF deduction)
  generally have zero tax liability and may not need to file — but this is an
  exemption from liability, not from registration; treat with caution in advice.

# CP500 (Advance Tax Instalments — non-employment income e.g. rental, business, royalties)

- Six instalments per Year of Assessment: due 31 Mar, 31 May, 31 Jul, 30 Sep, 30 Nov,
  31 Jan (following year)
- Each instalment must be settled within 30 days of the date shown on the LHDN notice

# Stamp Duty (STSDS Self-Assessment Regime — effective 1 Jan 2026)

- Malaysia moved to a self-assessment system: the duty payer (not LHDN) is now
  responsible for identifying chargeable instruments, calculating duty, filing
  returns, and paying — this is a significant shift in liability from prior years.
- Late stamping penalty: greater of RM50 or 10% of deficient duty, if stamped within
  3 months after the deadline
- Late stamping penalty (beyond 3 months): greater of RM100 or 20% of deficient duty
- Any business signing commercial/financial documents regularly (tenancy agreements,
  loan instruments, service agreements) should treat this as an active monthly process,
  not an annual afterthought.

# Notes for Agent Reasoning

- "Revenue" thresholds above refer to annual revenue, not profit — a common confusion
  point worth flagging explicitly when responding to SME owners.
- e-Invoice phase and CP500/Form B deadlines are independent tracks; a business can be
  in-scope for one and not the other depending on income type and revenue band.
